\small School of Science and Engineering (SSE), The Chinese University of Hong Kong, Shenzhen}

\begin{abstract}
Deep reinforcement learning provides a natural framework for sequential portfolio choice, but financial samples are short and joint tail dependence is difficult to learn from returns alone. We propose a synthetic-data-assisted portfolio controller that combines a fully dynamic, all-tree Student-$t$ D-vine with an LSTM-based Twin Delayed Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (TD3) agent. Edge-specific neural networks update the D-vine correlations from lagged marginal and dependence information, while fixed edge degrees of freedom preserve identifiability. The fitted training-prefix model generates 1,000 synthetic 24-month episodes for pre-training; the agent is then fine-tuned once on 61 realised historical trajectories. The policy maximises multi-period terminal-wealth CRRA utility subject to a scenario-CVaR penalty, transaction and financing costs, and self-financing long--short constraints. In a frozen evaluation of seven US and Chinese equity indices and gold, the preregistered mean-weight ensemble of 20 independently trained policies is compared on the same realised path with equal weight, constrained shrinkage mean--variance, DCC--GARCH, static and rolling vines, and a dynamic neural vine optimiser without RL. Over the 22 periods satisfying the preregistered completeness rule, the ensemble achieves the highest observed Sharpe ratio (2.018) and lowest volatility (13.19\%), but ranks third in total return and annualised CRRA certainty-equivalent return. Its utility advantage over equal weight is positive but not statistically significant under paired circular-block bootstrap inference. Post-holdout explanatory ablations show that a coarse no-vine TD3 ensemble performs better descriptively and that historical fine-tuning adds little on this sample; these findings motivate a preregistered matched-seed causal decomposition of vine features, scenario-CVaR information, synthetic pre-training, recurrence, and reward shaping. The evidence supports competitive risk and implementation efficiency, rather than statistically proven superiority or an established incremental vine effect.
\end{abstract}
